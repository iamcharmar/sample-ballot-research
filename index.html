School vouchers / education savings accounts

What this policy is—and what it actually changes

Texas Senate Bill 2 (SB 2, 2025) created the Texas Education Freedom Accounts program. Unlike public-school funding, it provides eligible families a state-funded account to pay approved private-school tuition, tutoring and other education expenses.

  • What became law: The state comptroller administers applications and approved providers. Home-school expenses have a $2,000-per-year cap; eligible private-school accounts are based on 85% of statewide per-student funding, with a higher amount allowed for qualifying children with disabilities.
  • How much and who gets it: The Legislature capped the 2026–27 biennial program at $1 billion. If eligible applications exceed funding, statutory priority groups and a lottery determine awards; approval is not automatic.
  • Real-world question: An award to a family already in private school is not evidence of a new transfer out of a public school.

School property-tax relief

What this policy is—and what it actually changes

A homestead exemption reduces the value of an owner-occupied primary residence subject to school-district property taxes. Texas voters approved a new school homestead exemption in November 2025; the amount became $140,000 instead of $100,000 for the 2025 tax year.

  • Specific change: The additional $40,000 is excluded from school-district taxable value. At a school tax rate of $1 per $100, this is an illustrative $400 annual reduction (40,000 ÷ 100 × $1); actual savings depend on the rate, eligibility and property value. Official adopted amendment.
  • School finance: Texas law provides additional state aid to offset eligible school-district tax revenue losses from the higher exemption; it does not send an equal cash payment to every homeowner. TEA school-tax guidance.
  • Limits: Renters do not directly claim a residence homestead exemption. An exemption does not by itself set the tax rate or prove how much total classroom spending changes.
